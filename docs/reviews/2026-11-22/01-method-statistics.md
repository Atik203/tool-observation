# Reviewer 1 — Method, identification, and statistics

- **Date:** 2026-11-22
- **Scope:** `paper/toolresults.tex` (all 1,945 lines), `writing-support/PROJECT_BRIEF.md`,
  `docs/GATES.md`, `prereg/PREREGISTRATION.md`, `docs/DECISIONS.md` (D-028–D-043), and the
  analysis/audit artifacts listed in the review brief.
- **Mode:** Read-only. I did not edit the paper or any artifact. I inspected schemas and
  aggregates, not raw trajectories or PII.

## 1. Verdict

**The fixed-prefix design supports a bounded, renderer-local, mover-weighted measurement of a
severity-ordered switch effect; it does not support the causal/content-sensitivity claim in the
title and abstract, and the recovery and silent-propagation secondary claims are not yet stable
enough to report as point estimates.**

## 2. What the design can and cannot support

The within-prefix contrast is credible for the question “does changing the prose observation change
the next decision, relative to the faithful prose paraphrase?” The design holds the prefix, task,
tool set, donor history, and policy fixed, excludes the raw-JSON V arm from the slope for a
documented format reason, and estimates a conditional log-odds with prefix-cluster inference. That
is a real measurement contribution.

It does **not** identify a general content-sensitivity effect. The treatment is the assigned
severity label, not verified criticality: 7/60 (majority), 11/60 (sheet-visible verifier), or
16/60 (blind verifier) of the audited critical-arm items are donor-unused, and the pre-registered
s2/s3 gate fails at 88.3%. The estimate is also local to one prose renderer, three 8–9B models,
two τ-bench domains, and greedy decoding. The paper says most of this in the discussion; the title
and abstract do not.
